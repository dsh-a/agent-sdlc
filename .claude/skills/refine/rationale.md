# Why `/refine` works this way

`SKILL.md` states rules. This file holds the measurements behind them, so a rule can be one line
there instead of a paragraph. **Read this when a rule looks arbitrary. Do not load it to follow
one** — it costs about 2,000 tokens and changes no procedure.

Every figure here was measured. Where a claim came from a log rather than a run, it says so.

---

## Probes, not topics

Step 3 was five sections of bullets under five personas: a developer, the codebase, a product
manager, an agile coach, a quality engineer. Across those sections were about **25 bullets, of
which five were probes** — an input, a question, a falsifiable verdict — and the rest were topics
(*"migrations, schema impact, ordering/uniqueness/cardinality"*).

A topic produces a finding only if the agent happens to think of one. Both findings from the one
live trial of the lenses came from probe-shaped bullets; the seven older categories produced
nothing new on that story. Reported at the time as the lenses working, the likelier reading is
that twenty bullets were diluting five.

Three structural faults decided the rewrite:

1. **No inputs.** The lenses took "the story" and returned "questions", so coverage could not be
   counted and no probe could be tested.
2. **`clean` was self-reported.** A lens never applied and a lens that found nothing printed the
   same word.
3. **A persona is a motive, not an index.** "Quality engineer" does not tell you what to point at.
   The personas survive in Step 3's opening paragraph because they are a good argument that the
   probe set is *complete*; they are no longer the structure.

The probe table is now generated by `probes.py probes`, and `framework_checks` asserts that
`SKILL.md` matches it. Two probes were merged while writing it — `two-engineers` into `ac-strict`,
and `sibling-mechanism` into `mechanism-exists` — because each pair asked nearly the same thing
of the same artifact, which is the dilution above, reappearing.

### Why the flags exist

Unflagged, `inherited-claim` wanted **47 verdicts** on one real story and `sibling-mechanism`
wanted 52. A gate that cries wolf is a gate whose failures get dismissed (`evidence` § tables).
Two changes made it tractable:

- **Artifacts dedup.** `#554` occurs 14 times in one body and is one dependency to judge.
- **Grounding flags.** A citation sitting next to a grounding marker is `grounded`, and the probe
  skips it.

Measured on the same story after both: 9 / 7 / 13 / 19 / 1 / 3 / 10 — about 60 verdicts total,
and `coverage --prior` carries forward every verdict whose artifact text is unchanged, so pass 3
pays only for what moved.

Flag precedence is deliberately asymmetric. `grounded` wins for a citation, because a citation
verified once is verified. `ungrounded` wins for a **number**, because proximity to the word
"measured" is weak evidence that *this* figure was.

## Verification by experiment (Step 2b)

Highest-yield act in the logs, and the skill had no step for it.

An epic specified test tags as `story:<issue>` and `release:<ver>`. Run, they are rejected:

```
$ flutter test --tags 'story:488'
Invalid tag name. Tags must be (optionally hyphenated) Dart identifiers.   # exit 1
```

`dart_test.yaml` rejects the same keys (exit 65), quoted or not. The taxonomy was unimplementable
as written, in **7 open issues**, and no amount of reading would have found it.

The inverse is on record too: one pass inferred from `--help` that `flutter test` ignored
`dart_test.yaml`, talked itself out of a correct worry, and was *disproved by experiment* — which
restored the original concern and changed the story. Hence the two negatives in the rule: reading
the documentation is not proving, and a missing `--help` flag is not proving.

The useful half of a proof is often negative. *A top-level `exclude_tags` is global and
inescapable, so it would break the workflow that runs that suite deliberately* ruled out an
entire design.

This pass produced one more, in the framework itself: `story.py load` reported `gate=ok` on a
story the board had marked **`Refined`**, because the gate compared against `REFINED`. Found by
running it against the live tracker. Nothing in the file would have shown it.

## Probes have been the culprit

One pass recorded *"Thirteen verification probes failed against zero content errors, and three
nearly published a false contradiction."* Another: *"Two agent 'contradictions' that did not
survive checking"* — the grounding subagent was wrong twice and the story body was right both
times.

That is the evidence behind two things: the log's required **Self-corrections** and
**Contradictions that did not survive checking** sections, and the decision **not** to spawn five
lens agents. More delegation buys more false contradictions, not more truth. Cost was the first
argument; this is the better one.

It is also why `evidence` § The discipline carries *a probe that disagrees with a settled figure
is the likelier suspect*.

## Grounding corrections

Every pass in the sample produced at least one:

- `ci.yml:41` → `:55`, and **wrong in the parent too**. Agreeing with the parent is a shared
  source, not corroboration — the `inherited-claim` probe exists for this.
- *"~8.5 min"*, inherited and never measured, against a real median of **12m50s over six runs**.
- A path renamed by a story that had since closed.
- Whole false premises: *"Q-11's premise was false"*, asserted by both the epic and the story.

A citation and its conclusion fail independently. A grounding subagent once attributed a sentence
to a GitHub issue's body when it had read it from a source comment one row earlier **in its own
report**; the phrase appears **0 times** in that issue, and the conclusion was sound. Checking
the conclusion would never have caught it.

## Vacuous satisfaction, and deferred numbers

*"Left at five, this story could have been marked satisfied while `perf` remained homeless"* —
the story enumerated five test suites where there were six, under an AC that read *"no suite can
pass by being silently skipped."*

`deferred-number` was found by running the quality lens over a story that had already had three
passes and read as READY. Its AC said *"older than an agreed threshold"* and named none — and the
thing it guarded was a cron that had failed **5/5 runs across 60 days** unnoticed, whose own
comment claimed daily while the schedule fired Mondays. An unnamed threshold is satisfiable by
one that never fires.

Both ACs read rigorous. That is why a human skim misses them and an enumerated probe does not.

## Seams

Both directions need the same kind of reason.

- **Split, justified:** three green flows were cut from one that is red by design against two
  open defects. Held together, three useful flows would have been unshippable until unrelated
  bugs landed.
- **Split, rejected, in the same pass:** a three-way cut, because *"a fixture child alone has no
  independently verifiable value."*

So `independently-demonstrable` asks whether a child can be shown without running its sibling,
and `SKILL.md` asks what *forces* the children apart rather than how many there are.

## Refusing to invent edges

*"Recorded so an edge is not added later by someone reading only the note."* Two stories touching
one subject are not dependent. The `named-edge` probe accepts `checked-none` as a verdict for
exactly this reason — an absence that was checked is a finding, and an absence that was assumed
is not.

Related: **one mechanism across siblings, not two.** Four stories needed one test-selection file
and none owned it, until a pass assigned it to one and had the others declare against it.

## Throwaways

Step 2b said to run the probe in a throwaway and paste its output. It did not say to delete the
throwaway, and a pass refining myapp #577 left `test/zz_probe_577_test.dart` behind — 3.2 KB of
print-only widget probes for chip overflow and `Semantics` tap handling, whose own first line read
*"THROWAWAY mechanism probe for /refine #577 — deleted after the run."*

It was still there after the pass finished, and `test/**` is the project's Test path glob, so a
bare `flutter test` swept it, counted it in the suite, and one `git add -A` would have committed a
print-only test into CI.

Two things are worth separating. The agent did the right thing — it proved the mechanism by
running it, which is the step working. The step was underspecified: a file is the *scaffolding* of
a proof and the pasted output is the proof, so the rule now names the deletion as part of
recording, not as a tidy-up afterwards. An instruction to clean up "later" is an instruction
nobody executes, because the pass ends first.

## Depth

`/cycle`'s own heuristics read the *argument string*. `/cycle BUG-088` matched every `hotfix`
condition while the work changed a ViewModel's lifetime across every entry path into a screen and
touched two layers.

`lean`'s cost is measured, not theoretical: fan-out 4 shipped **two regressions traceable to
inline AC**, and `verify` passed **11/11** because it audits coverage of *stated* criteria and
structurally cannot see a criterion nobody stated. That is why the rule is to choose `full`
whenever you hesitate — `full` costs one extra human gate.

Every fan-out through round 11 ran `--mode lean` for all five clones, because it was the only
setting that did not park each orchestrator on a question. Depth on the story is what removed
that forced choice.

## Counting

Predicted item counts were wrong **three times in one session while the content was right**. Only
an enumeration told the arithmetic error apart from an edit defect. So nothing in this skill or
in `evidence.py` accepts an expected total: `enumerate` reports each item's disposition, and
`coverage` reports each pair's.

## Customer profiles

Nothing in the five team perspectives asks who the story is for. All five ask whether it is
well-formed.

When `profile-impact` was added, a search for the product's own archetypes across the app repo
and the docs vault returned **zero** matches — the profiles existed only in the product owner's
head. So the probe ships with a contract (`customer-profiles.template.md`) and reports
`unavailable` when the file is absent.

That distinction is the point. `pitfalls.py` ran in every cycle in every repo for months
reporting `unparsed=6` while nothing read the number, and an inert gate reads as a passing one.
Could-not-determine is never absent.

## Why `SKILL.md` is still 566 lines

It was 753. The plan for this rewrite estimated ~320, which was wrong, and the measurement is
worth recording so the next estimate is better.

What left: ~280 lines of mechanism, into `probes.py` and `story.py`; the grounding prompt, the
log template and the closing verdict block, into files the agent `cat`s rather than retypes; the
war stories, into this file.

What stayed, and why none of it compresses further: nine probes with their questions (the probes
*are* the step), three confirmation gates in the split procedure, the two restatement blocks the
user reads, and the DoR table. The remaining fenced blocks are output contracts, not examples.

The honest accounting is that the line count was never the main cost. One invocation of this
skill now runs a probe table that a test asserts is complete and a DoR gate that exits 1 naming
what is unmet — where before, both were sentences.
